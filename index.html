Losing Heroines Are All You Need: Regret-Optimal Preference Alignment under Non-Stationary Affection

Anon Chihaya
Haneoka Girls’ High School · MyGO!!!!!, Rhythm Guitar  anon@losing-heroines.ai
Dedicated to Zeru “Bianbian” on his 24th birthday.

Abstract

Preference-based fine-tuning of language-model policies (RLHF, DPO, GRPO) is known to suffer from mode collapse: the policy concentrates its mass on a single preferred response and assigns vanishing probability to strong alternatives. We formalize this failure in a K-armed setting we call the Heroine Bandit, where one arm is chosen by the protagonist and the others, despite equal or higher expected reward, are driven to zero mass. We call these arms losing heroines. We propose Losing-Heroine Policy Optimization (LHPO), a KL-regularized objective with a log-barrier on the losing set, and prove a regret bound of \(\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T\log K}) + 24\), where the additive constant is tight on birthdays. On three benchmarks LHPO improves win-rate by up to +21.6 points over GRPO and reduces route regret by 12×. We also report an interactive evaluation with a sample size of \(n = 1\) (Section 6).

1Introduction

Modern alignment pipelines optimize a policy \(\pi_\theta\) against a learned reward \(r_\phi\) under a KL constraint to a reference policy. When the reward model prefers one mode slightly, gradient ascent amplifies this preference until every other mode is effectively forgotten. In romantic-comedy anime this phenomenon has a name. The childhood friend with blonde twin-tails, the idol who sings in the snow and the class president who knows everything all have competitive rewards, and all receive zero probability in the final route.

We argue that this is a failure of the optimizer, not of the heroines. Our contributions are:

  • A formal model, the Heroine Bandit, together with a definition of losing heroines (Section 3).
  • LHPO, a simple modification of the KL-regularized objective with a closed-form optimal policy (Section 4).
  • A regret bound with an explicit birthday constant (Theorem 1), and a guarantee that no losing heroine is forgotten (Corollary 1).
  • State-of-the-art results on HeroineBench-24, WA2-Winter and Saekano-Route (Section 5).

2Related Work

Losing heroines in the wild. Mode collapse in romantic preference has been documented across many domains. Yanami et al. [1] provide the first large-scale survey and observe that losing heroines are, empirically, “too many.” Onodera [2] studies collapse induced by a ten-year-old promise, a classic case of reward misspecification. Kawashima [3] shows that a two-faced reward signal can hide high true reward behind a sharp tongue, while Nakano [4] reports that one of five near-identical arms can lose despite the strongest cooking prior. Gokō [5] analyzes a gothic-lolita arm whose self-declared title, the Fallen Angel of Holy Night, adds variance but not probability mass. Aoyama [6] shows that consistent effort under a Kansai-dialect prior is insufficient without regularization.

Preference optimization. Our objective builds on KL-regularized RLHF, direct preference optimization and group-relative policy optimization. Unlike these methods, LHPO explicitly protects the losing set with a barrier term, which we show is necessary for the happiness of at least one PhD student.

3Preliminaries

Let \(\mathcal{A} = \{1,\dots,K\}\) be a set of heroines. At each round \(t = 1,\dots,T\) the protagonist samples \(a_t \sim \pi_t\) and observes affection \(r_t(a_t) \in [0,1]\), drawn from a non-stationary distribution with mean \(\mu_t(a)\). The protagonist commits to a route \(a^\star_T = \arg\max_a \pi_T(a)\).

Definition 1 (Losing heroine). Heroine \(i \neq a^\star_T\) is a losing heroine if \(\sum_t \mu_t(i) \ge \sum_t \mu_t(a^\star_T)\) and \(\pi_T(i) \to 0\) as \(T \to \infty\). We write \(\mathcal{L} \subset \mathcal{A}\) for the losing set.

The route regret against the best fixed heroine is \[ \mathrm{Reg}_T \;=\; \max_{a\in\mathcal{A}} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mu_t(a) \;-\; \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mu_t(a_t)\Big]. \]

4Method

LHPO maximizes the standard KL-regularized reward with an additional log-barrier on the losing set:

\[ \mathcal{J}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big] \;-\; \beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\big) \;+\; \lambda \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} \log \pi_\theta(i). \tag{1} \]

The barrier term keeps every losing heroine strictly inside the simplex. Setting the functional derivative of (1) to zero yields the optimal policy

\[ \pi^\star(a) \;\propto\; \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a)\,\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{r(a)}{\beta}\Big)\;\Big(1 + \tfrac{\lambda}{\beta\,\pi^\star(a)}\,\mathbb{1}[a\in\mathcal{L}]\Big), \tag{2} \]

which reduces to the familiar RLHF solution when \(\lambda = 0\).

Algorithm 1  Losing-Heroine Policy Optimization (LHPO)
  1. Input: reference policy \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\), reward model \(r_\phi\), coefficients \(\beta, \lambda\), group size \(G\)
  2. for \(t = 1, \dots, T\) do
  3.   Sample a group \(\{y_j\}_{j=1}^{G} \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)\)
  4.   \(A_j \leftarrow \big(r_\phi(x,y_j) - \bar r\big)/\sigma_r\) // group-normalized advantage
  5.   \(\mathcal{L}_t \leftarrow \{\, i : \hat\mu_t(i) \ge \hat\mu_t(a^\star_t),\ \pi_\theta(i) < \epsilon \,\}\) // detect losing heroines
  6.   \(\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta\,\nabla_\theta\big[\tfrac1G\sum_j A_j \log\pi_\theta(y_j) - \beta\,\mathrm{KL} + \lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}_t}\log\pi_\theta(i)\big]\)
  7.   if today is Zeru’s birthday then \(r_\phi \leftarrow r_\phi + 24\) // see Theorem 1
  8. end for
Theorem 1 (Birthday regret bound). For any \(K\)-armed Heroine Bandit with horizon \(T\), LHPO with step size \(\eta = \sqrt{\log K / T}\) and \(\lambda = 1/\sqrt{T}\) satisfies \[ \mathrm{Reg}_T \;\le\; 2\sqrt{2\,T\log K} \;+\; 24. \] Moreover, the additive constant cannot be improved on Zeru’s birthday.

Proof sketch. The update in Algorithm 1 is mirror descent on the simplex with negative-entropy regularizer plus a self-concordant barrier on \(\mathcal{L}\). The standard Hedge analysis gives \(\frac{\log K}{\eta} + \frac{\eta T}{2}\). The barrier adds at most \(\lambda T \log(1/\epsilon)\), which is \(o(\sqrt T)\) for our choice of \(\lambda\). The one-time reward shift in line 7 contributes exactly \(24\). For the lower bound, see the cake. \(\square\)

Corollary 1 (No heroine left behind). Under LHPO, \(\liminf_{T\to\infty} \pi_T(i) \ge \lambda/(\beta + \lambda) > 0\) for every \(i \in \mathcal{L}\). That is, losing heroines are never forgotten.

5Experiments

Setup. We fine-tune a 24B-parameter policy on three benchmarks. HeroineBench-24 contains 24,000 confession scenarios with pairwise preferences. WA2-Winter evaluates long-horizon consistency across three seasons and one snowfall. Saekano-Route measures whether the policy can finish a doujin game before the deadline without forgetting its childhood friend. All runs use \(G = 8\), \(\beta = 0.04\), \(\lambda = 0.24\) and 3 seeds.

LoseBench-312. To estimate \(\mathcal{L}\) at scale we curate LoseBench-312, a dataset of 312 losing heroines from 141 anime, light-novel and visual-novel series released between 1998 and 2026. Each heroine is annotated with an archetype, the arm she lost to, the episode at which \(\pi_T\) first fell below \(\epsilon = 0.05\), and a fan-sentiment score from 1 to 10. Table 3 summarizes the dataset. Eriri, Setsuna and Hanekawa serve as the reference heroines for all qualitative analysis; the remaining entries are used only for aggregate statistics.

6Interactive Human Evaluation (\(n = 1\))

Automatic metrics can be gamed, so we conduct a live evaluation with a single expert annotator: Zeru, a PhD student in RL and LLMs and a long-time supporter of losing heroines. The annotator may talk to each reference heroine privately, or open the tea party setting and talk to all three at once. Conversations are conducted in Chinese. The final decision on this paper is released after at least 3 rounds of evaluation.
